# Repair robots

`Assets/Prefabs/RepairRobots/FinderBot` and `SkateBot` contain the complete robot prefab, its repair definition, and its own `Parts`, `Meshes`, and `Materials` folders. The five part names are unchanged: Wheel, Battery, Headlights, Back_Body_Robot, Window_Robot. Each family has different prefab references, so parts cannot be installed on another robot family. HUD labels include the family name. Wireframe previews include view-dependent silhouettes for smooth panels and distribute their line budget between meshes, so dense wheel rims cannot erase other parts.

`Assets/Resources/RepairRobotCatalog.asset` adds these robots to the existing SCAV_BOT repair dropper without modifying the scene. Six main orders cycle through the three families; endless orders choose a family randomly. Repair bots are delivered through orders; the old F/manual-spawn action is retired and hidden from Controls without changing saved binding indices. Rewards, the completed-order counter, salvage progression, pipe delivery, and repair tools use the selected robot's parts.

In the developer console, `parts` lists all IDs. Examples: `spawn finderbot_wheel`, `deliver skatebot_battery 2`. Original IDs such as `wheel` still refer to SCAV_BOT parts.

Fitting a carried part gently attracts it toward a nearby aligned slot, then animates its geometry from the hand into the mount. Loose parts have small positional and angular play; the visible progress of every fastener reduces that play, and tightened fasteners bias its pivot toward the secured side. The last completed fastener locks the exact original pose. Backing a fastener out restores play and marks the robot unfinished again. Wheel/body hammer seating is retained. Installation temporarily disables the part's body colliders and fastener interaction; debugging/resetting repair restores the original pose and collider states. `RepairPartMount` exposes fitting duration, positional play and angular play on fitted parts. These effects add no audio.

`RepairRobotRevival` is installed on every repair delivery. A fitted battery briefly pulses its LED and any fitted lamps; a fully secured battery powers the robot. Secured headlights fade into an idle glow and run a brightness check; FinderBot also scans its beams slightly. Each secured running-gear module performs a short visual self-test, and a completed set checks together once. SCAV_BOT rotates its round wheels; FinderBot and SkateBot use small suspension movements for their long, single-piece running-gear meshes. Power arriving last queues checks for modules already secured. Backing out power or module fasteners cancels the affected checks; retightening permits a fresh check. These effects use instance property blocks and temporary render-only wheel proxies, preserve slot/collider/fastener poses and shared materials, add no audio, and stop wheel motions immediately when the crane reserves a robot. Timing, wheel angle and lamp brightness are editable on the runtime component.

`RepairRobotFinalTest` automatically runs a 3.4-second power, optics and running-gear inspection when the repair is complete. With empty hands, interact with the robot to repeat it or diagnose unfinished work. Missing modules, unseated parts and unfinished fasteners are reported in English/Russian; nearby faults receive crisp amber HUD points. Loose modules rattle slightly more during inspection, loose optics flicker amber, and unsecured running gear stutters. A stable completed robot ends with `[OK] ROBOT READY`. Repair completion/rewards remain unchanged; the crane can interrupt the inspection immediately. The test adds no audio. Fully tightened fasteners lose both persistent and aimed outlines after their final turn finishes; backing them out restores their highlight.

Fastening uses pointer gestures. Engage with LMB; while engaged, mouse motion works the tool and the camera stays steady. A wrench stroke starts right to tighten or left to loosen, then releasing LMB resets the wrist; Shift is not required. The direction is latched for that stroke, so returning across spent travel while held cannot gain extra progress. The old Shift + rightward loosening stroke remains compatible. The screwdriver follows small clockwise pointer circles to tighten and counter-clockwise circles to loosen, with angular assistance. Final-order hints explicitly ask for leftward wrench strokes or counter-clockwise screwdriver circles. RMB leaves the work position. Stationary holds, clicks, scroll and straight screwdriver drags do not tighten anything. The final 18 degrees have increased resistance and only a small remaining head/washer travel. A normal fastener takes about two or three wrench strokes.

Most modules have two fasteners, including separate lamps grouped into one pair; large body panels keep four. Round wheels use two opposed hub bolts, while long running-gear modules use two fore/aft side fasteners. First bring the fasteners to 60% snug. After the first corner, the farthest opposite fastener is marked by a crisp amber HUD dot; adjacent corners wait until the opposing pair is caught. Once that pair supports the panel, catch the remaining corners, then perform the final clamping turns. Uneven pressure tilts the part toward the secured corner; opposite support levels and steadies it. Motion controls, stroke travel, assistance and final resistance are exposed on RepairWrench. No sound is added.

To rebuild the generated assets after changing the FBX models, use Tools > Moon Game > Repair > Rebuild FinderBot, SkateBot and Zombie. Rebuilding replaces generated prefabs and meshes; save any custom changes separately first.

# Zombie

`Assets/Resources/Zombie.prefab` contains the complete Cosmonaut model, a CharacterController, procedural movement and pose, and ten bone-following hit zones. Use `spawn zombie` in the F3+F4 console. It requires a clear floor near the human operator; it does not spawn enemies automatically.

The zombie detects a visible human, follows the last seen position, steers around nearby obstacles, and strikes/shoves at close range. It has no dependency on a baked NavMesh. It does not introduce a player health or death system.

The prefab's ZombieActor component exposes health, speed, detection/attack range, attack interval, and corpse lifetime. Each ZombieHitZone exposes its body part and damage multiplier. Default health is 100; PlayerPistol bulletDamage is 34. Head x3, torso x1, arms x0.55, legs x0.7. Leg hits slow movement. Hits briefly interrupt walking and produce a directional flinch; reactions fade over 0.65 seconds. Death stops the AI and locomotion capsule, enables a ten-body physical ragdoll with constrained joints, transfers movement and the shot impulse to the struck bone, and removes the corpse after 35 seconds. Bone colliders continue to collide with the environment and receive physical impulses from later shots. The ZombieRagdoll component exposes deathImpulse; bone Rigidbody masses and CharacterJoint limits are editable in the prefab. Impact particles are pale suit debris and dust, without blood. Gunshot audio stays unchanged.

# Lamps

BreakableLamp discovers the existing emissive `lamp` glass in each loaded scene and adds a thin hit collider. Each nearby point light is assigned to one glass by position, including lights parented under a different lamp. A pistol shot hides the struck glass, emits pale glass fragments, and switches off its light. Other lamps stay on. Metal housing is retained; this is runtime damage and resets when the scene reloads.
